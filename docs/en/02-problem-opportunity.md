
# 02 — Problem and Opportunity

**SAFE FOOD AI — Product, Consumer Experience, and Opportunities for Innovation with Artificial Intelligence**

The analysis begins with consumer uncertainty surrounding the communication of dietary needs in food delivery orders, exploring the gaps between recording specifications, interpreting them, and fulfilling them at the establishment.

This chapter expands on the foundations presented in the project overview, connecting the identification of a real need to the formulation of innovation opportunities, value hypotheses, and validation criteria.

The approach considers consumer experience as its starting point, **solving real-world problems** as its guiding principle, operational communication as its area of intervention, and artificial intelligence as a strategic tool for interpreting and organizing information, developing contextualized solutions, and supporting human decision-making.

**Nature of the analysis:** Problem investigation, consumer experience, product discovery, opportunity analysis, value hypotheses, and proposed indicators.

---

## 2.1 Defining the Core Problem

SAFE FOOD AI emerged from the identification of a communication gap between consumers and food establishments, particularly in orders requiring customization.

Based on this observation, the problem was defined from the perspective of consumer experience.

The main difficulty does not necessarily lie in the inability to communicate a dietary specification, but in the uncertainty surrounding how that information will be received, interpreted, and considered by the establishment.

Consumers may record their needs in a special instructions field, but this record alone does not confirm that the instructions have been understood or that fulfilling them is feasible.

This lack of predictability may create uncertainty, particularly when an order involves needs requiring specific attention.

### Problem Statement

The ability to record dietary specifications in food delivery orders does not ensure that consumers have clarity about whether their requests are understood and fulfilled by the establishment.

From a product development perspective, this situation highlights a difference between an available feature and the need it is intended to address.

**Recording an instruction does not necessarily mean establishing understandable and verifiable communication.**

SAFE FOOD AI builds on this distinction to investigate how more structured information, contextualized guidance, and automated processes may contribute to a more predictable consumer experience.

### Product Discovery Perspective

The problem was formulated around three considerations:

1. **Human need:** Consumers want their individual requirements to be understood and respected.
2. **Operational challenge:** Establishments need to interpret information that may involve varying levels of complexity.
3. **Technological opportunity:** Intelligent systems can support the transformation of this information into more organized and contextualized guidance.

The proposal does not assume that artificial intelligence can eliminate consumer uncertainty.

The objective is to investigate whether improved communication can help reduce some of the uncertainties that shape this experience.

---

## 2.2 Dimensions of Consumer Uncertainty

Uncertainty related to dietary specifications does not necessarily manifest in the same way for every consumer.

Its nature depends on individual needs, order characteristics, and the information provided by the establishment.

To understand this diversity, the problem was organized into four dimensions.

### 2.2.1 Uncertainty About Understanding the Instructions

Consumers may provide detailed instructions without knowing whether those instructions have been interpreted as intended.

This difficulty may be particularly relevant in orders involving multiple items, different quantities, and exclusions that apply only to specific units.

Information may be recorded correctly and still introduce ambiguities during interpretation.

In this context, clarity depends not only on the amount of information provided, but also on how it is organized and presented to the recipient.

### 2.2.2 Uncertainty About Order Fulfillment

Even when instructions are understood, consumers may not have enough information to know whether the establishment is capable of fulfilling them.

The feasibility of customization depends on factors such as ingredient availability, internal procedures, and operational capacity.

Structured communication can support the assessment of these conditions, but it does not replace confirmation from the establishment.

This dimension demonstrates that understanding a request and being able to fulfill it are separate stages.

### 2.2.3 Uncertainty About Operational Precautions

Some specifications require attention beyond removing or replacing an ingredient.

Assembly, identification, packaging, and transportation may also influence whether an order meets consumer expectations.

The example of esfihas presented in Chapter 01 illustrates this relationship: in addition to identifying the units requested without cheese, operational guidance may consider keeping them separate during packaging and transportation.

This recommendation does not guarantee the absence of cross-contact, particularly in cases involving food allergies.

The identified opportunity lies in broadening the understanding of order requirements by considering different operational stages without assuming that technology can verify all of them.

### 2.2.4 Uncertainty About the Need for Confirmation

Not every request can be fulfilled solely on the basis of the information initially provided.

In situations involving unknown ingredients, potential allergens, or operational limitations, human confirmation becomes essential.

Without this confirmation, important questions about order feasibility may remain unresolved.

This dimension also establishes a principle for the responsible application of artificial intelligence: **when the available information does not support a reliable conclusion, uncertainty must be acknowledged rather than replaced with an apparently definitive answer.**

The four dimensions represent an analytical framework developed to guide the project. They have not yet been validated through formal consumer research.

## 2.3 Needs of the Stakeholders Involved

Although consumer uncertainty is the central problem, reducing it also depends on the operational conditions of food establishments. Even when establishments intend to accommodate individual needs, logistical factors, limitations in process organization, and internal communication failures may make it difficult to interpret, communicate internally, and fulfill requests.

In this context, SAFE FOOD AI recognizes that these challenges are not necessarily associated with a lack of attention from establishments, but also with the complexity of transforming individual instructions into coordinated actions across the different stages of an order.

The proposal seeks to address this gap by using artificial intelligence and automation to support information organization, facilitate operational communication, and contribute to problem-solving, without replacing human judgment and responsibility.

For this reason, SAFE FOOD AI considers the complementary needs of both participants in the experience.

### 2.3.1 Consumer Needs

Consumers want their individual requirements to be understood and respected.

In this context, their needs may include:

- Communicating dietary specifications clearly.
- Distinguishing modifications that apply to each item in an order.
- Reducing the possibility of misinterpretation.
- Having greater predictability regarding how instructions are communicated.
- Knowing when a request requires additional confirmation.
- Perceiving attention and care in how their needs are handled.

The product proposal focuses on improving the communication of this information, rather than certifying that an order will be prepared as requested.

### 2.3.2 Establishment Needs

Establishments need to receive understandable information to assess feasibility and organize order fulfillment.

Their needs include:

- Identifying items, quantities, and exclusions.
- Understanding customization requests.
- Distinguishing ingredient exclusions from explicitly declared allergies or intolerances.
- Recognizing potentially relevant operational precautions.
- Identifying situations that require clarification.
- Maintaining human responsibility for food preparation and food safety decisions.

These needs highlight the relationship between consumer experience and operational communication.

The proposed solution considers that better-organized information may support establishments, but does not assume that using it will automatically result in requests being fulfilled.

### 2.3.3 Relationship Between Needs and Value Creation

SAFE FOOD AI recognizes that the same technological intervention may generate different benefits for each stakeholder.

For consumers, the potential value relates to information clarity and communication predictability.

For establishments, it relates to organizing instructions and supporting the interpretation of requests.

This relationship guides the product's positioning: **consumer experience is the central motivation, while operational communication represents the solution's primary area of application.**

## 2.4 Order Journey and Friction Points

The consumer experience was analyzed as a sequence of interactions, from defining dietary specifications to receiving the order.

The objective was to identify the stages at which ambiguities, communication difficulties, or additional verification needs may arise.

The journey presented is a conceptual analytical model, not a mapping validated through direct consumer observation.

| Stage | Consumer Expectation | Potential Friction Point |
|---|---|---|
| Food selection | Identify options compatible with individual needs | Insufficient information about ingredients and customization |
| Recording specifications | Communicate instructions | Extensive, ambiguous, or poorly structured notes |
| Receiving instructions | Have the information understood | Lack of confirmation that the instructions were understood |
| Feasibility assessment | Know whether the order can be fulfilled | Limitations not clarified by the establishment |
| Preparation and assembly | Receive items as requested | Incorrect interpretation of quantities or modifications |
| Identification and packaging | Keep items differentiated | Confusion or contact between items with different specifications |
| Transportation | Preserve appropriate order conditions | Lack of information about precautions taken |
| Order receipt | Verify that the order meets the specifications | Late identification of discrepancies |

### Friction Point Analysis

The journey demonstrates that communication challenges do not end when an instruction is recorded.

Information needs to remain understandable throughout the stages required to fulfill the order.

However, not every friction point can be resolved by an artificial intelligence system.

Some depend on ingredient availability, internal procedures, food handling conditions, and human decisions.

For this reason, the opportunity for intervention was limited to stages in which technology can directly support the interpretation, organization, and communication of information.

The current scope of SAFE FOOD AI focuses on interpreting specifications and generating and communicating operational guidance.

The MVP does not directly monitor or verify food preparation, packaging, transportation, or delivery receipt.

## 2.5 Potential Consequences of the Problem

Communication difficulties may produce different consequences depending on the nature of the specifications and the establishment's operational conditions.

### 2.5.1 Consumer Experience and Trust

Uncertainty about whether requests will be fulfilled may generate concern, frustration, and emotional strain.

When discrepancies occur, consumers may need to seek clarification, request corrections, or choose not to consume the food received.

These possibilities justify investigating mechanisms that contribute to more understandable communication.

From a user experience perspective, the problem also involves the difference between the expectations formed when placing an order and the experience ultimately received.

### 2.5.2 Operational Efficiency

Ambiguous instructions may require additional clarification and increase the need for manual verification.

In certain situations, misinterpretations may contribute to rework, waste, or dissatisfaction.

Organizing information represents an opportunity for operational support, although its benefits still need to be evaluated under real-world conditions.

### 2.5.3 Food Safety

In orders involving declared allergies, improper interpretation of information may be associated with significant risks.

However, there is a fundamental distinction between communicating a need and ensuring that it is safely accommodated.

Generating a dietary alert does not verify ingredients, food handling procedures, kitchen conditions, or the absence of cross-contact.

These responsibilities remain with the establishment and require specific food safety practices.

### 2.5.4 Implications for Product Development

The potential consequences indicate that the product should prioritize information fidelity and transparency about its limitations.

This means that solution quality should not be evaluated solely by its ability to generate responses, but also by its ability to preserve original instructions, avoid inappropriate classifications, and recognize situations requiring human assessment.

**Scope of the analysis:** The consequences presented represent conceptually identified risks and possibilities. The project does not have commercial data or consumer studies that would allow their frequency to be quantified.

## 2.6 Identified Opportunity

The product opportunity consists of transforming dietary specifications provided by consumers into information that is more structured, understandable, and useful to establishments.

As presented in Chapter 01, the SAFE FOOD AI proposal goes beyond reproducing the original order notes.

The product investigates the possibility of combining structured interpretation, generative artificial intelligence, and automation to support the communication of individual needs.

This approach is based on one principle: **the application of artificial intelligence should address an identified need rather than serve as an end in itself.**

The opportunity is organized into three complementary areas.

### 2.6.1 Organizing Specifications

Structured interpretation seeks to identify relevant information and preserve its relationship with the corresponding items.

This includes quantities, modifications, exclusions, and explicit declarations of allergies or intolerances.

This stage aims to reduce ambiguity without assigning dietary conditions that the consumer has not declared.

Fidelity to the original information is an important requirement, as subsequent recommendations depend on accurately interpreting the recorded needs.

### 2.6.2 Contextualized Operational Guidance

Generative artificial intelligence is used to develop recommendations related to the specific characteristics of each order.

Guidance may address preparation, assembly, identification, packaging, transportation, and verification needs.

The objective is to complement the original communication with suggestions that may be useful for human assessment by the establishment.

Applying AI makes it possible to explore recommendations adapted to the order's context, but also introduces the need to evaluate the relevance, consistency, and limitations of the generated responses.

Recommendations do not replace food safety protocols or guarantee that the order meets the requested specifications.

### 2.6.3 Automated Communication

Automation connects information interpretation to the presentation of dietary alerts.

In the MVP, results are sent to a demonstrative restaurant viewing interface and a WhatsApp test destination.

This workflow demonstrates technical integration between system components without representing official communication with restaurants registered on food delivery platforms.

### 2.6.4 Conceptual Differentiation of the Solution

The SAFE FOOD AI proposal lies in combining complementary capabilities within a single workflow.

Structured interpretation organizes information; generative artificial intelligence expands the context of the guidance; and automation enables results to be sent to configured destinations.

This combination distinguishes the concept from a conventional special instructions field, without assuming proven superiority over existing solutions.

The potential value lies not only in AI-generated text, but in transforming an individual need into clearer operational information while maintaining explicit boundaries for automated processes.

## 2.7 Product Value Hypotheses

SAFE FOOD AI was developed based on hypotheses connecting more structured communication to potential improvements in consumer experience and establishment operations.

These hypotheses represent expectations to be investigated, not outcomes that have already been demonstrated.

### Hypothesis 01 — Communication Clarity

**If** dietary specifications are organized into structured alerts, **then** establishments may be able to interpret instructions more clearly.

**Expected benefit:** Reduced ambiguity related to items, quantities, and exclusions.

**Validation criterion:** Compare the interpretation of orders presented in a conventional format with the interpretation of the same orders after information structuring.

### Hypothesis 02 — Perceived Predictability

**If** consumers use a structured process to record their needs, **then** they may perceive greater clarity regarding the information communicated to the establishment.

**Expected benefit:** Improved perception of organization and predictability.

**Validation criterion:** Investigate participants' perceptions through user experience tests and structured questionnaires.

This hypothesis does not amount to confirmation that the restaurant received, accepted, or fulfilled the requests.

### Hypothesis 03 — Broader Consideration of Precautions

**If** generative AI produces contextualized recommendations, **then** it may help identify operational precautions that were not explicitly described in the original notes.

**Expected benefit:** More comprehensive guidance for establishment assessment.

**Validation criterion:** Evaluate recommendation relevance based on defined scenarios and human review criteria.

### Hypothesis 04 — Reduction of Discrepancies

**If** instructions are communicated more clearly, **then** discrepancies associated with their interpretation may be reduced.

**Expected benefit:** Lower frequency of errors related to order customization.

**Validation criterion:** Compare the occurrence of discrepancies under representative operational conditions.

This hypothesis requires operational validation and cannot be demonstrated through technical tests alone.

### Hypothesis 05 — Automation Feasibility

**If** structured interpretation and guidance generation are integrated into an automated workflow, **then** it will be possible to produce and send dietary alerts without manual intervention at every processing stage.

**Expected benefit:** Demonstration of technical feasibility and potential for workflow evolution.

**Validation criterion:** Monitor request completion, failure handling, and the delivery of results to configured destinations.

Automation does not eliminate the need for human oversight in decisions related to food safety.

### Hypothesis 06 — Trust in Responsible Communication

**If** the solution preserves consumer-provided information, distinguishes explicit declarations from inferences, and acknowledges uncertain situations, **then** it may contribute to a perception of more responsible communication.

**Expected benefit:** Greater confidence in how information is handled, without creating a misleading perception of guaranteed safety.

**Validation criterion:** Assess whether users understand the system's capabilities, limitations, and need for human confirmation.

This hypothesis recognizes that a responsible experience should communicate not only what technology can do, but also what it cannot verify.

## 2.8 Proposed Validation Indicators

To investigate the hypotheses presented, indicators were defined to guide future product evaluations.

The selection considers three complementary dimensions:

1. **Consumer value:** Clarity, predictability, and perceived trust.
2. **Solution quality:** Information fidelity, recommendation relevance, and appropriate handling of uncertainty.
3. **Operational feasibility:** Interpretation efficiency, technical reliability, and alert delivery.

The indicators do not represent results measured to date.

| Dimension | Proposed Indicator | Evaluation Method |
|---|---|---|
| Communication clarity | Rate of correct instruction interpretation | Comparison between original orders and evaluator interpretations |
| Information fidelity | Preservation of items, quantities, and exclusions | Tests using reference cases |
| Consumer experience | Perceived clarity and predictability | Participant survey following interaction |
| Trust | Perceived trust in communication | User questionnaire |
| Operational efficiency | Time required to understand instructions | Comparative tests with and without structured alerts |
| Recommendation quality | Relevance and applicability of guidance | Human evaluation using defined criteria |
| Interpretation safety | Frequency of inappropriate allergy or intolerance classifications | Tests involving affirmative, negative, and ambiguous statements |
| Technical reliability | Rate of completed processing and handled failures | Request monitoring |
| Alert delivery | Delivery rate to configured destinations | Technical logs and available confirmations |
| Understanding of limitations | Ability to recognize situations requiring human confirmation | Participant comprehension tests |

### 2.8.1 Criteria for Interpreting Results

Evaluation should distinguish technical indicators from indicators related to product experience and impact.

Successful processing of an order does not demonstrate that the restaurant fulfilled it correctly.

Likewise, technical delivery of an alert does not demonstrate that it was read, understood, or followed.

Consumer perceptions of trust should also not be interpreted as evidence of food safety.

Investigating the solution's actual value will require evaluations with participants and, in the future, validation in representative operational environments.

### 2.8.2 Validation Prioritization

Considering consumer uncertainty as the central problem, initial experience evaluations should prioritize information clarity, understanding of system limitations, and perceived predictability.

In parallel, technical evaluation should prioritize information fidelity and the prevention of inappropriate classifications.

This prioritization seeks to prevent product evolution from being guided exclusively by technological performance indicators without considering the value perceived by users.

## 2.9 Available Evidence and Opportunity Limitations

MVP development produced evidence related to the solution's technical feasibility.

This evidence must be distinguished from value hypotheses that have not yet been validated.

### 2.9.1 Implementation Evidence

The MVP includes:

- An order and dietary specification submission form.
- Structured interpretation of the information provided.
- Differentiation between ingredient exclusions and explicit declarations of allergies or intolerances.
- Gemini integration for contextualized recommendations.
- Automation through Make.
- A demonstrative restaurant viewing interface.
- Alert delivery to a WhatsApp test destination.
- Error handling, timeouts, and request cancellation.

The implementation demonstrates a workflow connecting information input, processing, guidance generation, and automated communication.

### 2.9.2 Technical Validation Evidence

The project records 37 automated tests covering order interpretation, alert API protection, and polling.

Local build, TypeScript, and lint checks were also performed, along with functional tests of the integrated workflow in the MVP environment.

These validations demonstrate specific aspects of the MVP's technical feasibility, while its potential impact on consumer experience, operational efficiency, and business value remains subject to future evaluation.

---

## 2.10 Opportunity Summary

The analysis presented in this chapter expands on the problem that motivated the development of SAFE FOOD AI.

Consumer uncertainty was identified as the primary pain point to investigate, particularly given the difficulty of obtaining predictability regarding the understanding and fulfillment of dietary specifications.

The product opportunity consists of supporting communication between consumers and establishments through information organization, contextualized recommendation generation, and automated operational alerts.

The MVP demonstrates the technical feasibility of integrating these components into a functional digital experience.

From a business innovation perspective, the project explores how artificial intelligence technologies can be used to address individual needs, support operational processes, and create opportunities to improve the consumer experience.

However, its ability to reduce consumer uncertainty, improve operational efficiency, or contribute to a more predictable experience still depends on validation with users and establishments.

Further development should be guided by hypothesis evaluation, results analysis, and feature refinement based on the evidence obtained.

**The central proposal of SAFE FOOD AI is to transform individual dietary needs into clearer information and practical guidance, supporting communication and human assessment without replacing establishments' responsibility for food safety and order fulfillment.**

This approach positions SAFE FOOD AI as a project applying artificial intelligence to business challenges, grounded in identifying a problem, building a functional solution, and continuously investigating its potential value.
