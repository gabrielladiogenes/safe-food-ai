
# 01 — Project overview and origin

**SAFE FOOD AI — Artificial intelligence applied to the communication of dietary needs**

**Project nature:** Functional MVP, digital product development, generative artificial intelligence, and process automation.

## 1.1 Contextualization

Food plays a role that goes beyond satisfying physiological needs. Meals are also associated with self-gratification, emotional memories, and social moments.

In this context, the experience of ordering food for consumers with specific dietary requirements is not limited to choosing an establishment or product; it also involves needs and expectations.

Allergies, intolerances, personal preferences, ingredient restrictions, and needs related to sensory predictability or cognitive rigidity require a level of safety that the traditional method of adding "notes" to orders cannot provide.

Although these conditions have different origins and implications, they share an operational challenge: the need to communicate specific instructions clearly, understandably, and in an actionable manner.

Uncertainty about whether these requests will be fulfilled can generate concern, frustration, and distress even before the order is received.

On the other hand, the perception of care, attention, and respect for the consumer's individual needs can contribute positively to the experience.

## 1.2 Problem and opportunity identification

SAFE FOOD AI emerged from a gap in communication between consumers and food establishments, particularly in orders requiring customization.

In many delivery systems, customers can specify exclusions or add notes, but the possibility of contacting the establishment to confirm receipt of these specifications is uncertain. Each restaurant's communication logistics ultimately become a major variable in determining whether the order information has actually been understood.

From this observation came the opportunity to investigate how generative artificial intelligence could serve as a layer of interpretation and operational support.

Rather than simply reproducing the notes written by the consumer, an intelligent solution could identify relevant specifications, organize the information, and propose contextualized precautions for each order.

This approach considers two participants in the experience:

**The consumer**, who wants their needs to be understood and their expectations respected.

**The establishment**, which needs to receive clear information and practical guidance to assess feasibility and fulfill the order.

The project is based on the hypothesis that more structured communication can reduce ambiguity, facilitate order verification, and contribute to a more predictable experience.

### Practical application: From specifications to operational guidance

Consider an order consisting of four chicken esfihas and six calabresa sausage esfihas.

The consumer specifies that one chicken esfiha and three calabresa sausage esfihas must be prepared without cheese.

In a conventional workflow, this information may remain limited to the order notes field, depending on the establishment's interpretation and procedures.

In the SAFE FOOD AI approach, the request is interpreted and reorganized into a structured dietary alert, highlighting quantities, flavors, and their respective exclusions.

In addition to communicating which units must be prepared without cheese, the system uses generative artificial intelligence to produce contextualized guidance that supports order fulfillment.

In this scenario, one of the recommendations, for example, is to package the esfihas without cheese separately from the traditional ones, reducing the possibility of contact between items with and without restrictions during transportation.

The recommendation demonstrates a central characteristic of the proposal: **going beyond the literal interpretation of an instruction by also considering the operational precautions necessary to fulfill it.**

When the dietary alert is generated on the platform, it is simultaneously sent to the "Restaurant View" simulator and to a linked WhatsApp test destination, simulating the delivery of the message to the restaurant associated with the customer's order.

This measure does not guarantee the absence of cross-contact and does not replace specific food safety protocols, especially in situations involving a declared allergy.

## 1.3 The birth of SAFE FOOD AI

SAFE FOOD AI was conceived as an intelligent bridge between consumers and food establishments.

The product combines interpretation rules, generative artificial intelligence, and automation to support the communication of needs related to food preparation, assembly, identification, packaging, and transportation.

Its purpose is to provide more organized information, preventive suggestions, and human verification points, while respecting the limitations of technology and the responsibilities of the professionals involved.

## 1.4 Project mission

**To promote clearer, more considerate, and more personalized food-related communication by using artificial intelligence to transform individual needs into practical guidance, support establishments, and contribute to more predictable and respectful consumer experiences.**

## 1.5 General objective

To develop and validate an MVP based on generative artificial intelligence and automation, capable of interpreting dietary specifications provided by consumers and producing contextualized alerts intended for establishments.

## 1.6 Specific objectives

- Structure order information containing different types of dietary specifications.
- Differentiate ingredient exclusions from explicit declarations of allergies or intolerances.
- Generate contextualized guidance on preparation, assembly, identification, packaging, and transportation.
- Integrate AI processing into an automated communication workflow.
- Display alerts in a demonstrative establishment interface and send them to a WhatsApp test destination.
- Implement mechanisms for error handling, timeouts, and request cancellation.
- Validate system behavior through automated tests and functional scenarios.
- Document decisions, results, and limitations to guide future product iterations.

## 1.7 Project scope

SAFE FOOD AI was developed as a demonstrative MVP, without official integration with delivery platforms and without validated commercial operations.

Specifications are provided individually for each order, without requiring registration, a persistent dietary profile, or consumer history.

I emphasize that the guidance generated by artificial intelligence does not replace ingredient verification, food safety practices, or human assessment by the establishment.
